People treat typography as a font choice. It is really a pair of questions you have to keep answering: how wide is the line, and how much air is between lines. Get those two right and almost any decent typeface will read well. Get them wrong and the most beautiful face on earth will feel like work.

## Measure

The line length is the *measure*. Too long and the eye loses its place on the return sweep. Too short and the rhythm breaks every few words. The old advice is roughly sixty-six characters per line, and the old advice is mostly right.

  This paragraph is set to a capped measure. Resize the window and watch the column refuse to grow past its limit. That refusal is the whole trick.
</aside>

I cap the reading column and let the margins take the rest. The text does not need to be wide. It needs to be easy to come back to, line after line.

## Rhythm

The space between lines is the *rhythm*. Body text wants more room to breathe than people expect, somewhere near 1.6 times the font size. Headings want less, because their job is to feel like a single gesture rather than a stack of separate lines.

> Set the body for comfort and the headings for confidence.

After that it is mostly restraint. One typeface for voice, one for the small technical marks like dates and code, and a single accent color used so rarely that it still means something when it appears.

That is the entire system. Two questions and a little discipline.
